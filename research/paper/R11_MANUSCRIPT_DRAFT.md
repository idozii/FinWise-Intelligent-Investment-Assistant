# Reliability, Calibration and Abstention in Financial Price Forecasting: An Audited Retrospective Case Study

**FinWise research manuscript — DRAFT 0.1, 28 October 2026. Not a submitted or accepted paper.**

**Status:** Historical M5-v2 first-run analysis plus exploratory R8/R9 development study. **No new independent R10 evaluation has been executed**; the R10 preregistration gate remains blocked. These results do **not** establish a new conformal algorithm, reliable future coverage, investment profitability, or model superiority.

## Abstract

Modern financial prediction systems often display a point forecast without providing sufficient evidence that the forecast is more accurate than a persistence baseline or that its uncertainty estimates are reliable after a selective reporting policy. We evaluate an explicitly simple standardized Ridge regression model against last-observed-price persistence and assess prequential interval calibration and width-based forecast abstention. A frozen retrospective external-asset first run using Tiingo raw close prices for GLD, IWM, XLE and XLV produces 64 forecast origins per asset/horizon, using one- and seven-session targets. Ridge has a higher observed mean absolute error (MAE) than persistence in all eight comparisons, although all eight descriptive moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals include zero. Ridge's nominal-90% ready-origin interval coverage reaches at least 90% in only two of eight groups. A separate development-only experiment comparing fixed rolling intervals with a delayed-feedback ACI-style calibration rule improves observed ready-origin coverage in 14 of 16 groups but increases average interval width in all 16. Under a 10% width-based abstention threshold, the adaptive rule sometimes accepts substantially fewer forecasts, including zero Ridge H7 forecasts for AAPL. We also identify a documented 2-for-1 XLE share split that is crossed by a forecast origin accounting for 22.5–24.9% of the total original XLE H7 absolute error, motivating careful corporate-action-aware estimand design. These findings document failure modes and trade-offs in reliability-aware forecasting, but they are descriptive and do not establish conditional coverage guarantees, causal effects or trading utility.

**Keywords:** prequential evaluation; time-series forecasting; uncertainty calibration; adaptive conformal inference; selective prediction; abstention; corporate actions; evidence provenance.

## 1. Motivation and research questions

**RQ1 (accuracy):** Does a time-respecting standardized Ridge predictor reduce absolute price forecast error relative to the last-close persistence baseline?

**RQ2 (marginal reliability):** How close are rolling-calibrated prediction intervals to their nominal 90% ready-origin empirical coverage across assets and horizons?

**RQ3 (availability):** When an interval-width abstention policy is applied, what happens to the fraction of forecasts reported and their empirical selected-sample coverage and error?

**RQ4 (data integrity):** How do corporate actions alter the economic interpretation of forecasts evaluated on unadjusted historical close prices?

Our contribution at this stage is a transparent **case-study protocol and failure-mode audit**, not a novel forecasting or conformal coverage algorithm. A new algorithmic or statistical research contribution, if any, requires stronger related-work differentiation and a genuinely independent R10 study.

## 2. Design and evidence classes

### 2.1 Predictors and time-respecting training

We compare persistence (forecast equal to last observed close) against a Ridge regression model with standardized, lagged numerical features and fixed regularization parameter alpha=10. The research pipeline refits on historical examples with matured targets at successive origins. We evaluate horizon steps H1 and H7. Point estimates and intervals were generated by separate research scripts, **not** by the public FinWise live ARIMA endpoint.

The M5-v2 protocol was fingerprinted before evaluation (`research/protocols/m5_tiingo_external_assets_v2.json`, locked SHA-256 `7c844332597962da3fa1fb68eba776279d695a19fe991cd545934fd3347f274a`). It fixed the two models, two horizons, 252-session minimum training history, step of 7, 30-residual calibration warmup, rolling window of 60, nominal alpha of 0.10 and interval-width acceptance threshold of 10%. Paired moving-block bootstrap summaries used block size 5, 1,000 iterations and seed 42; these 95% intervals are explicitly descriptive, not multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory tests.

### 2.2 First retrospective external-asset replication (M5-v2)

The M5-v2 asset set is GLD, IWM, XLE and XLV, derived from authenticated Tiingo EOD **raw Close** source snapshots. Its source range is 27 October 2021 through 27 October 2026 inclusive, with forecast origins restricted to 1 January 2025–27 October 2026 and all targets inside the window. The first run had 64 eligible evaluated forecast origins per asset/horizon. R6/R11 audits preserve the recorded provider source SHA-256 values, dates and original results.

This is an **independent-instrument historical first run with provider amendment**, **not a prospective market-time evaluation**. It follows an earlier exploratory development study on AAPL/MSFT/SPY/TSLA and must not be pooled casually with that study or rerun after inspecting errors as if independent.

### 2.3 Development-only calibration and abstention experiments (R8/R9)

The R8 development input consists of pre-existing M2.1 origin-level predictions on AAPL, MSFT, SPY and TSLA, 143 origins per asset/model/horizon. Both calibration methods use the same point forecasts:

- `fixed_rolling`: rolling matured absolute errors and a fixed nominal miscoverage parameter.
- `delayed_feedback_aci_style`: similar matured-error intervals with an alpha update, `alpha <- clip(alpha + 0.02*(0.10 - 1{miscoverage}), 0.01, 0.50)`, performed only when **a previously issued interval's label** matures. Because this variant clips alpha, handles overlapping labels by delayed feedback, and applies empirical selection, published adaptive-conformal theorems are **not** assumed to apply.

R9 explores post-hoc interval-width thresholds 5%, 10%, 15%, 20% and 30% on the **same development origin predictions**. Decisions use interval width available at each origin, not its eventual realized error. Each cell reports acceptance relative to all forecast origins and ready origins, empirical ready and selected coverage, and selected relative MAE. Comparisons of Ridge and Naive on selected origins use only *jointly accepted identical origins*.

The five-cutoff sweep is an **exploratory policy sensitivity study**; choosing a preferred threshold after inspecting R8/R9 does not create an independently validated selection policy.

### 3.2 Calibration and abstention on external assets

Ridge empirical coverage over the 64 ready origins reached at least nominal 90% in **2/8** asset/horizon cells (IWM H7 93.75% and XLE H7 90.625%). GLD coverage was 78.125% (H1) and 76.5625% (H7). The time-dependent 64-origin estimates are descriptive and are not a formal assessment that a finite-sample exchangeability guarantee was violated.

For XLE H7, Ridge accepted **0/64** origins using the frozen 10%-width threshold; the Naive interval policy accepted **14/64**. The selected MAE and selected coverage of Ridge are therefore undefined—not zero.

A selected-origin comparison of GLD H7 and IWM H7 finds negative Ridge-minus-Naive MAE differences when restricting to **jointly accepted** origins (GLD: −0.2477, n=52; IWM: −0.2514, n=13). These are smaller, conditional subsets and **do not reverse the all-origin first-run result**.

### 3.3 R8 development: improving coverage, widening intervals

Across 16 development groups (4 assets × 2 models × 2 horizons), delayed-feedback adaptive intervals produced higher ready-origin coverage than fixed rolling in **14**, equal coverage in **2**, and wider mean relative intervals in **all 16**. Only **5/16** adaptive groups reached ≥90% empirical ready-origin coverage. Neither theoretical coverage validity nor out-of-sample superior reliability follows from these exploratory observations.

For MSFT Ridge H7, both methods had 88.5% ready-origin coverage, but selected coverage was 82.5% for fixed rolling (n=40) and 70.6% for the ACI-style method (n=17). The difference in accepted origins and small adaptive sample preclude a matched-policy causal interpretation. This gap between *ready-origin coverage* and *selected coverage* illustrates why selective reporting requires its own evaluation.

### 3.5 Corporate-action integrity (R6)

A single XLE H7 forecast originating 2 December 2025 and targeting 11 December 2025 crossed a 2-for-1 XLE share split effective **5 December 2025**. The event was announced **20 November 2025**, prior to the forecast origin. The user's R6 read-only audit attributes **24.9% of total XLE H7 Naive absolute error** and **22.5% of Ridge error** to that one crossing origin. Original M5 scores were **not changed**.

Source: [State Street issuer announcement, 20 November 2025](https://investors.statestreet.com/investor-news-events/press-releases/news-details/2025/State-Street-Investment-Management-Announces-Share-Splits-for-Five-Select-Sector-SPDR-ETFs/default.aspx).

A raw Close price-drop across a split is not automatically a comparable economic investment loss. Adjusting target definitions *post hoc* after inspecting test errors would contaminate claims of independent evaluation. A future study must specify in advance whether the estimand is raw, split-neutral or dividend-adjusted total return, and what split/dividend information was available at each forecast origin.

## 4. Related work

The persistence forecast is a foundational, hard-to-beat benchmark in financial price forecasting. Hyndman and Athanasopoulos emphasize benchmarking against naïve random-walk forecasts rather than selecting a complex predictor solely for sophistication ([Forecasting: Principles and Practice, Ch. 5](https://otexts.robjhyndman.com/fpp3/toolbox.html)).

Gibbs and Candès study adaptive conformal inference under distribution shift and online calibration with local adaptation ([JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/beta/papers/v25/22-1218.html)). This work is **not equivalent** to our clipped, delayed-feedback baseline, and its published guarantees are not claimed here.

Bao et al. develop CAP for online selective conformal prediction and false coverage-statement rate control ([JMLR 2025](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/24-0452.html)). Sale and Ramdas analyze conditions and flaws in some selective-conformal guarantees and describe corrected calibration-set approaches ([TMLR 2025](https://mcml.ai/publications/sr25a/)). These existing methods mean that selection-aware calibration by itself **cannot be claimed as FinWise's novelty**. A substantive method contribution would need comparisons and mathematical conditions matching the independent test design.

## 5. Validity, limitations and ethics

1. **No prospective test:** M5 is a historical held-out instrument replication. R8/R9 are exploratory development analyses. R10 is blocked.
2. **Multiple comparisons and dependence:** eight M5 paired bootstrap intervals are descriptive and not adjusted for multiple tests. Sequential overlapping price errors may have dependencies beyond the chosen bootstrap block.
3. **Selection conditioning:** comparing observed selected losses across methods operating on different origin subsets is not a causal estimate. Selected empirical coverage is not automatically nominal coverage.
4. **Corporate actions and source revisions:** Tiingo raw Close may undergo mechanical discontinuities due to splits. Retrospective adjusted data can encode future events; proper point-in-time data availability needs a separate estimand and provenance protocol.
5. **Proxy mismatch:** empirical MAE and interval coverage are not transaction cost, execution feasibility, risk-adjusted return, actual recommendation utility or safe trading behavior.
6. **Representativeness:** small asset set, limited historical regime coverage, common market exposure and snapshot-date choices limit generalizability. Event and vendor details should be audited before release.
7. **Public data rights:** no raw vendor market bars, token, or redistribution-restricted source files may be published without verifying licensing. Aggregates still require review under the provider contract.
8. **No novel algorithm claim:** the present contribution is evidence-centered integration and audit. Publication eligibility requires careful literature differentiation and a convincing new scientifically testable statement.

**Manuscript conclusion (provisional):** On these observed datasets, Ridge did not improve all-origin MAE over persistence, while adaptive-style calibration increased observed coverage but also widened intervals and reduced forecast availability in selected cases. Corporate-action auditing is necessary for interpreting raw-close price errors. We document these results transparently as motivation for a fully precommitted, truly independent future study rather than as proof of a superior market forecasting system.
